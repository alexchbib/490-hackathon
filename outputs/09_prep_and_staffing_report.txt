====================================================================================================
[TRACK A: HOW MANY UNITS OF EACH PERISHABLE SKU SHOULD EACH BRANCH PREP TOMORROW?]
====================================================================================================
Decision rule (newsvendor): prep the q-th quantile of tomorrow's demand, where
  q = critical ratio = Cu / (Cu + Co),  Cu = unit gross profit lost on a stock-out,
  Co = unit cost wasted on an unsold item = Unit Cost x (1 - salvage share).
With zero salvage, q equals the item's gross margin -> Garden's 1.46x cost directly lowers its optimal prep.

Scope: 22 perishable SKUs x 3 branches = 66 branch-SKU series, 24,259 units (44.9% of all units).
Demand model: Poisson GLM  log(mu) = SKU-at-branch + DayOfWeek + trend   (train rows 9,966, test rows 1,980; mean June demand 2.51 units/SKU/day)
  June forecast MAE: model 1.278 | lag-1 1.860 | lag-7 1.719 | 4-week same-weekday avg 1.418 units

Assumptions & limits (Track A):
  * Recorded sales = true demand. If items sold out in history, demand is under-counted (censored) and every rule under-preps.
  * No substitution: a customer facing a sold-out SKU buys nothing. In reality many switch to a sibling SKU, which shrinks
    the cost of stock-outs AND the absolute $ gaps between rules. Treat the $ figures as an upper-range simulation;
    the ranking of rules and the Garden-vs-Harbour prep difference are the robust results.
  * Achieved service levels exceed targets because demand is a small integer (~2.5/day): the optimal prep is the
    smallest whole unit whose cumulative probability reaches the ratio, which overshoots it.
  * Prep is decided once per day per SKU with no batch-size constraint; real bakery batches (e.g. trays of 6) blunt the gain.
  * Waste value = Unit Cost from the data. If Garden's 1.46x cost is an accounting allocation (Finding 1), its true waste
    cost is lower and its critical ratio should be closer to Harbour's — the audit in Finding 1 also sets this parameter.
  * Practical test: run R7 at one branch for 4 weeks with daily waste and sell-out-time logs; compare waste $ and lost sales to R3.
