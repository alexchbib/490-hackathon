[STEP 2: WHAT A MENU PRICE RISE IS WORTH, AND HOW PRICE-SENSITIVE CUSTOMERS WOULD HAVE TO BE TO MAKE IT LOSE MONEY]
====================================================================================================
Base: June 2026 run-rate revenue $2,088/day ($762,145/yr, no further growth assumed); gross margin 58.7%
Formula: change in GP = Revenue x [(1 + e x p)(m + p) - m], e = elasticity, p = price rise, m = margin.
Break-even elasticity e* = -1 / (m + p): any customer response milder than e* makes the rise profitable.

====================================================================================================
[STEP 3: STAGED +5% RISE — PILOT AT GARDEN, THEN ALL BRANCHES — SIMULATED ON REAL NOISE]
====================================================================================================
Metric: daily log(units at Garden / units at Harbour+Uptown), adjusted for month x weekday.
Its day-to-day noise in Jan-Jun: SD 0.142 (~14%), lag-1 autocorrelation -0.10.

Plan: raise Garden +5%; compare with the 8 weeks before, relative to the other branches; check weekly.
  Stop rule: roll back as soon as we are >=80% confident the volume loss exceeds Garden's break-even (9.6%).
  At week 12: keep Garden if the estimated loss is below its break-even; otherwise roll back.
  Simulation: daily noise is resampled from the real Jan-Jun residuals; the true effect is injected; repeated 4,000 times per elasticity. 12-month GP is compared with changing nothing.
  Extension rule: at week 12 the rise goes to each other branch only if the measured loss is inside THAT branch's
  break-even (Garden 9.6%, Harbour 7.1%, Uptown 7.4%): Garden's low margin makes it the
  most tolerant branch, so passing at Garden alone is not enough for Harbour or Uptown.

Reading the table:
  * If customers respond like elasticity -0.5, the staged plan earns about $21,440 in 12 months.
  * Across every elasticity tested (down to -4, far beyond what any data here suggests), the staged plan's average
    outcome is never worse than a $942 loss (worst 5% of runs: $8,178): the stop rule
    caps the downside at a few weeks of one branch.
  * 'Raise everywhere now' earns ~$6,077 more if customers ignore price (the price of caution), but
    loses $10,482/yr at elasticity -2 and $34,777/yr at -3.
  * The pilot does NOT measure elasticity precisely; it only answers 'is the loss worse than break-even?',
    which is the only question the decision needs.

Assumptions & limits:
  * Revenue base is June's run-rate with no further growth; growth would raise every $ figure proportionally.
  * Volume response is immediate and stable; slow erosion (customers drifting away over months) would be caught late.
    Mitigation: keep the weekly comparison running for 6 months after rollout.
  * The other branches are a valid comparison: no simultaneous promotions, menu changes or local events at Garden.
  * Customers do not switch branches because of the rise. If Garden customers move to Harbour, Garden's volume
    falls but chain profit does not; the pilot would read this as price sensitivity and be conservative.
  * Elasticity is uniform across items. Item-level rises (e.g. only Matcha & Tea, 59-82% margin) need a separate test.
